Public Service Delivery Stack

Every public service a citizen receives — a water connection, a vaccine certificate, a wage payment, a building permit — is the visible tip of a much deeper system. Beneath it sits shared digital infrastructure: registries, workflows, payment rails, notification engines. And beneath that, something less visible but more consequential: the policies, programmes, institutions, and human networks that determine whether any of it works at all.

Delivering public services in a genuinely citizen-centred way requires all three layers working together — the Foundations that make change sustainable, the Digital Building Blocks that make it scalable, and the Products and Interfaces that make it tangible for citizens. Most reform programmes invest almost entirely in the top layer. The evidence from two decades of field work is consistent: the foundations are what determine whether a reform lasts.

The gap is measurable. Only 39% of citizens in surveyed countries trust their government to deliver services reliably (OECD, 2023). Fewer than 1 in 3 countries are on track for accountable public institutions by 2030 (UN SDG 16.6). Over $1 trillion is lost annually to fragmented, inefficient public administration (World Bank, 2022).
